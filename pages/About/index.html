You are not alone with scoliosis.

We are Nashley and Nadia Adams, identical twin sisters who were both diagnosed with scoliosis at 12. We started the NA Scoliosis Foundation to give people in South Africa the encouragement, awareness and support we wish we had earlier.

What we do

Gifts of hope

We pack and give “gifts of hope” bags to people newly diagnosed with scoliosis, so they know someone is on their side.

Raise awareness

Many people have never heard of scoliosis until a doctor mentions it. We share what it is and what living with it is like.

Raise funds

Through donations, sponsorships and community events, we aim to help patients who need urgent medical procedures.

Our mission

To build awareness, understanding and acceptance of scoliosis in our communities, so that everyone living with it can feel confident, valued and at home in society.

Our vision

A South Africa where scoliosis is spotted early, nobody faces it alone, and every patient is supported in body and spirit.

“Embrace your inner spine, because you are beautiful no matter what anyone says.” Nashley & Nadia

Our story

We were 12 when we found out. While we tried on costumes for our school jazz concert, our mother noticed something wasn’t right with Nadia’s back. After X-rays at Red Cross Children’s Hospital, Nadia was diagnosed with adolescent idiopathic scoliosis. I, Nashley, was checked next and had the same diagnosis. Nadia’s spine curves in an S, mine in a reverse S.

At first the hospital was frightening. Doctors spoke about curves, degrees and numbers we didn’t understand. Over the years the X-ray team came to know us (“here come the twins!”), and that made check-ups easier. Seeing children with more severe scoliosis, some in wheelchairs, stayed with us.

In high school, now at Groote Schuur Hospital, we covered our backs so nobody would notice. Eventually we stopped caring what others thought, and we found out who our real friends were. We were lucky to have loving parents, supportive teachers and friends. Many people don’t, and that is why we started this foundation.

What people with scoliosis told us

We surveyed other people living with scoliosis to learn what would make them feel valued. Three things came up again and again.

  • Few people understand itRespondents said there is little awareness of scoliosis.
  • Many feel left outMany felt excluded and alone in what they were going through.
  • Hard experiences are commonMany had dealt with bullying and low moods.
